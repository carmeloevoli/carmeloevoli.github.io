## High-Energy Astroparticle Physics – Theory (2026/2027)

### Synopsis

High-energy astroparticle physics studies the most energetic particles in the Universe (cosmic rays, gamma rays and neutrinos) and the sources that produce them. This course develops the theory needed to interpret these observations. It follows one population of particles from the sources where they are accelerated, through the magnetized interstellar medium where they propagate, to the radiation they produce along the way.

The course has three parts:

1. **Radiative processes** (lectures 1–5). How relativistic electrons and nuclei lose energy and radiate: synchrotron and inverse Compton emission, gamma-ray absorption, and hadronic interactions with gas and photons. These processes connect cosmic rays to the gamma rays and neutrinos we observe.
2. **Cosmic-ray transport** (lectures 6–11). Why cosmic rays diffuse through the Galaxy. Starting from the basic properties of astrophysical plasmas, we derive the cosmic-ray transport equation and obtain the diffusion coefficient from the scattering of particles by magnetic turbulence. We then use them to interpret measurements of nuclei, electrons, positrons and antiprotons.
3. **Particle acceleration** (lectures 12–15). How particles reach such high energies: the general requirements for acceleration, the physics of shock waves and supernova remnants, and diffusive shock acceleration with its observational tests.

### Prerequisites

Classical electrodynamics, special relativity, statistical mechanics, and basic particle physics and astrophysics. No background in plasma physics is assumed: the plasma physics and magnetohydrodynamics needed in the course are introduced from scratch.

### Exam

There are two lists of problems: one on radiative processes, one on transport and acceleration. Each student picks one problem from each list, solves both individually, and presents the solutions at the blackboard.

### Lectures

15 lectures of 2 hours.

#### Part I – Radiative processes

- **Lecture 1**. Introduction and steady-state solutions with radiative losses
    - Cosmic rays, gamma rays and neutrinos: the multi-messenger view
    - Course and exam organization
    - Kinetic equation for particles with radiative losses
    - The *cooling* break
- **Lecture 2**. Synchrotron radiation
    - Synchrotron power from a single particle
    - Relativistic aberration
    - Characteristic synchrotron frequency
    - Synchrotron radiation from an electron population
- **Lecture 3**. Inverse Compton scattering
    - Inverse Compton scattering in the Thomson regime
    - Electron energy losses through inverse Compton scattering
    - Relation between incident and scattered photon energies
    - Inverse Compton emission from an electron population
- **Lecture 4**. Klein–Nishina effects and gamma-ray absorption
    - Transition to the Klein–Nishina regime and its consequences
    - Lepton cooling in the Milky Way
    - Photon–photon pair production, optical depth and the gamma-ray horizon
- **Lecture 5**. Hadronic interactions and diffuse gamma rays
    - Proton–proton interactions and pion production
    - The neutral-pion gamma-ray spectrum and the pion bump
    - Diffuse Galactic gamma-ray emission
