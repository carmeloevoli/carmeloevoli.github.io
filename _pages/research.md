My work is theoretical and phenomenological: I build models of how cosmic rays are accelerated and
transported, and confront them with the data returned by direct-detection experiments, air-shower
arrays and gamma-ray and neutrino observatories. Four themes organise most of what I do.
A curated list of papers is on the [publications page](/publications/); the codes I maintain are
listed there as well.

## Galactic Cosmic Rays: transport and phenomenology ##

How cosmic rays propagate through the Galaxy, and what the spectra we measure at Earth tell us
about their sources. My main interest is in *non-linear* transport, where the particles are not
passive tracers of a fixed magnetic turbulence but generate the waves that scatter them. This
self-confinement picture connects the shape of the observed spectra, the size of the diffusive
halo, and the anisotropy — quantities usually treated independently. On the phenomenological side I
work on the interpretation of precision data from AMS-02, and on the origin of the knee.

## Ultra-High-Energy Cosmic Rays: composition ##

At the highest energies the key observable is not the spectrum alone but the *mass composition*,
inferred indirectly from the depth of shower maximum. I work on both ends of that inference: the
air-shower parametrisations that turn measurements into statements about composition, and the
propagation of nuclei through intergalactic magnetic fields, where self-generated turbulence and
the structure of the magnetised cosmic web can reshape both spectrum and composition before the
particles reach us. I am a member of the [Pierre Auger Collaboration](https://www.auger.org).

Recent work:

## Gamma and neutrino astrophysics: diffuse emissions ##

Cosmic rays are only directly observable near Earth, but the gamma rays and neutrinos they produce
in interactions with interstellar gas map their distribution across the whole Galaxy. Diffuse
emissions are therefore a transport diagnostic, and a demanding consistency test: any model of
Galactic propagation must reproduce the spectra measured locally *and* the diffuse sky seen by
Fermi-LAT, LHAASO and IceCube simultaneously. Much of my recent work is about whether the current
picture passes that test.

Recent work:

+ *The origin of very high-energy diffuse γ-ray emission: The case for galactic source cocoons*,
Astronomy & Astrophysics **698**, L18 (2025) [[arXiv]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14651)
+ *LHAASO protons versus LHAASO diffuse gamma-rays: a consistency check*, MNRAS Letters **543**, L20
(2025) [[arXiv]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06593)
+ *Scrutinizing the cosmogenic origin of the KM3-230213A event: A Multimessenger Perspective*
[[arXiv]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11993)

## Physics beyond the Standard Model in Astroparticle ##

Astrophysical environments reach energies and densities no laboratory can, which makes them
sensitive probes of new physics. I use cosmic-ray and neutrino observations to constrain
super-heavy dark matter, primordial black holes and axion-like particles — work that runs through
my earlier interest in how energy injected by exotic particles affects the early Universe.

Recent work:

+ *Exploring memory-burdened primordial black holes with ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays*,
Physical Review D **114**, 043034 (2026) [[arXiv]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15827)
+ *Constraining superheavy dark matter with the KM3-230213A neutrino event*,
Physical Review D **113**, 043024 (2026) [[arXiv]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08779)
